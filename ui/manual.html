Load a .kicad_pcb board, open the Placement panel, and choose a preset. Step advances the solver and previews the result; Accept writes the previewed pose into the working board. Run runs one pass to the iteration budget (max_iterations) and previews the result; accept (or run again) from there.

Reading a placement run

SignalMeaning
convergedMotion is below the settle threshold and net force is quiet. Safe to accept.
stalledNo net progress for the stall window, but forces remain. A part is wedged or chattering; move a neighbor or add room.
budgetThe iteration cap was reached while work remained. Treat it like stalled, not settled.
emptyNothing movable or nothing to integrate.
legal / overlapPost-hoc courtyard and board-boundary check on the final merged pose.

Pinned parts and locked footprints are anchors: they apply forces but never move. Ghosts are non-copper anchors — walls that push parts out of keepouts, or attractors that pull a named target.

Labels ride along

Each footprint's silkscreen reference label (F.SilkS / F.Fab) is a low-mass node sprung to its parent part's center — free float inside a small dead zone (label_slack_m, 0.5 mm), 40 Hz spring beyond — integrated alongside the pads, never welded text. The spring fetches labels in next to their parts; the label's clearance rect (text bbox plus a font-height halo) collides through the same wall law as courtyards and always wins, so ink ends clear of copper and visible after assembly. The node is hard-clamped to its own disc (label_orbit_m, 3.5 mm reach, grown past big parts so the disc always includes clear space beside them). When a parent rotates, its label snaps to the nearest readable orientation (0/90/180/270 — 180 survives for mirrored bottom-side text). label_clearance_m = 0 parks every label at its filed spot (orbit off); any positive value enables orbit. Parent footprints never feel label forces, so pad trajectories match label-unaware runs. Normative formulas: SI force laws.
